# V1.2-E — nested budgets, deadlines and cancellation

Status: **implemented** (2026-09-19; CI recorded below once the pull request has run)
Recommended model effort: **alto**
Repository: `bOps`
Depends on: V1.2-D complete
Governing ADR: `docs/architecture/adr/0030-privilege-reducing-multi-agent-delegation.md`

## Outcome

Make budgets, deadlines and cancellation hold across nested work (ADR-0030 section 6).

## Checklist

- [x] Failing tests first: exhausted step/token budget, deadline expiry, cancellation during each role and during a side-effecting step, cancellation while awaiting approval.
- [x] Child budget and deadline are reserved from the parent's remaining amount and reconciled at role end.
- [x] D checks the run's deadline before each role starts and ends the run as `DeadlineExceeded`, and leaves every role's `Consumed` empty; this task counts a role's steps and tokens, stops a role at its own deadline while it runs, reconciles `BudgetConsumed` and ends a run on an exhausted step or token budget. Cancellation still propagates as an exception from `DelegationRunner.StartAsync`; ending the run as `Cancelled` and the cancellation token tree are here.
- [x] Count a role's steps and tokens and stop at its deadline. V1.2-C2 deliberately leaves them out of the step path: the envelope check in `ExecuteStepAsync` covers tool, risk, window and Skill scope, and using up a granted budget or reaching the deadline is `BudgetExceeded` or `DeadlineExceeded`, not a denial of authority (ADR-0031 §3). The delegated entry points do not yet enforce them.
- [x] One cancellation token tree covers run, role and step; each end state is a distinct audited terminal state.
- [x] A cancelled side-effecting step with an unknown outcome is recorded as unknown, not as failed. As an audit event (`DelegationJournalAuditEvent`, outcome `Cancelled`); the durable intent and outcome entries are V1.2-F.

## Definition of Done

- [x] Budgets, deadlines and cancellation are proven across nested work.
- [x] Restarting a role never resets its budget. A role's meter and scope start from a prior `BudgetConsumption` (tested), and the run reserves the next role from what earlier roles spent; the restart itself is V1.2-F.

## Interpretations made in E

Where ADR-0030 section 6 and ADR-0031 leave a detail open, E chose as follows. None changes an ADR.

1. **A step is what a role does, not what the model says.** One step is one iteration of a model role's loop (a tool call
   or the final answer), one step of an approved plan, one verification read and one evidence call a Capability makes.
   The planning, replanning and empty-answer retry calls are not steps, but their tokens are counted.
2. **Tokens overshoot by at most one call.** A call cannot be stopped half way, so a role stops after the call that takes
   it over its tokens (a budget of exactly the tokens spent is not exceeded). The check runs after the plan, after a tool
   step and after a replan, like the runner's own `MaxTotalTokens`, so a final answer already produced is not thrown away;
   what it cost is charged and the next role finds less left.
3. **The run's budget is the sum of its roles', as C1 derived it, and roles run one after another.** A role's ceiling is
   `min(what the run has left, its profile, the request)`. Nothing left is `BudgetExceeded`, not a denial (ADR-0031 section 3), and
   only steps count for a role that makes no model call: an empty token budget does not stop Remediation or Verification.
4. **A plan is applied whole or not at all.** Before a human is asked, the Remediation role must have at least as many steps
   left as the plan has; otherwise the run ends as `BudgetExceeded` with nothing changed. What can still stop a plan half way
   is a deadline or a cancellation, which the unknown-outcome record and V1.2-F's reconciliation are for.
5. **The deadline is the instant itself.** A role or run whose deadline is `now` is expired, as in D. The run's deadline also
   bounds the wait for a plan approval, so a human who never answers cannot hold a run past it. A deadline further than
   30 days away is left to the clock checks at each step, because a timer cannot hold that long.
6. **Cancelling a run under way returns it.** It ends as `Cancelled`, with the active role `Cancelled`, and is returned; D let
   the exception through. A token that is already cancelled when `StartAsync` is called still throws, because no run exists
   to end. The writes that record an ending never use the cancelled token, so an audit sink that refuses one still records it.
7. **A role that hits a deadline or its budget is `Failed`; a cancelled one is `Cancelled`.** The run's status carries the
   distinction, as in D. An interruption that is neither the caller's nor a deadline is `Failed`.
8. **Verification that runs out of steps or time is not `VerificationFailed`.** It confirmed nothing (rule S4) and ends the
   run as `BudgetExceeded` or `DeadlineExceeded`, so an operator can tell a refuted change from an unfinished check.
9. **Unknown is an audit event for now.** A non-Read step cancelled while it runs is audited with `StepOutcomeKind.Cancelled`
   and no `ToolCallAuditEvent` of a failure; the run's error says the outcome is unknown. A Read step has no effect to be
   unknown, so it writes none. The intent entry, the durable journal and the reconciliation that settles it are V1.2-F.
